# Module: Billing and payments

> Addresses **payment costs** (card processing), **payouts and reconciliation**, and **cash flow** (interest and late fees). It often delivers results quickly and is a practical first module because it can help fund the rest of the project.

## 1. Card processing fees (2 weeks)

1. Collect the last 3 months of statements from each payment processor and calculate the **effective rate** by payment type: in-person card, online card, and any other method you accept.
2. Compare like with like. For a US worked example, Square lists in-person processing at **2.6% + 15¢** and online processing at **2.9% + 30¢** on its Free plan. Rates vary by country and card type; for example, Stripe lists standard UK cards at **1.5% + 20p** and standard EEA cards in Germany at **1.5% + €0.25**. See the [global market research](../../mercados/global-pesquisa-2026-10.md).
3. Request proposals from 2–3 processors **with your statements in hand**; then give your current provider a chance to match the best offer.
4. Consolidate terminals or providers when each handles only a small volume; concentrating volume may improve your negotiating position.
5. Check for terminal rental, setup fees, and other charges. Confirm locally whether card surcharges are permitted: rules differ by market.

## 2. Payout timing and financing (only when needed)

- Check whether payouts are being accelerated automatically under your contract. Some businesses may not realize they are paying for this.
- Turn off automatic advances if you do not need them; use financing only to cover a real cash-flow need.
- If you need financing, compare offers from the processor and other available providers. Rates and terms vary; verify locally.

## 3. Reconciliation

Check weekly: sales recorded in the POS × expected processor payouts × deposits received in the bank; marketplace payouts × orders. Turn discrepancies into a follow-up list for the processor or platform. Track the reasons for refunds and chargebacks so you can address the cause, such as late delivery or missing items.

## 4. Local instant payments

- **Direct orders:** Offer card payments and, where available, a local instant bank payment option. FedNow, Faster Payments or Pay by Bank, and SEPA Instant are examples of systems or options in some markets; merchant availability, customer experience, and fees need local verification.
- **Automatic confirmation:** If your payment provider supports it, use a payment link or dynamic payment request that confirms automatically, so staff do not need to check screenshots.
- **Recurring orders:** If the local payment provider supports recurring payments, consider subscriptions such as weekly lunches, meal boxes, or corporate breakfasts. Confirm the payment method, customer authorization, cancellation rules, and fees locally.

## 5. Cash-flow forecast

Create an 8-week spreadsheet: expected inflows (processor settlement schedule, marketplace payouts) × outflows (suppliers, payroll, taxes, rent). Flag any week projected to go negative, before you need overdraft financing.

## 6. Collecting from customers who pay later

For local company accounts or trusted regular customers, replace informal tabs with invoices or payment links that include a due date and automatic reminders. Use SMS or email only in line with applicable consent and privacy rules; requirements vary across the US, UK, EU, Canada, and Australia.

## Implementation paths

**A — no new software:** negotiation + reconciliation spreadsheet + cash-flow forecast. This can deliver much of the benefit.

**B — with a payment provider:** use a provider that supports payment links, automatic payment confirmation, and any locally available recurring or instant-payment options; connect it to your own ordering system where possible.

## Checklist

- [ ] Last 3 months of statements from each processor and marketplace
- [ ] Current effective rate by payment type (spreadsheet)
- [ ] 2–3 proposals compared; owner’s decision recorded
- [ ] Automatic payout advances reviewed
- [ ] Weekly reconciliation routine assigned (who, when)
- [ ] 8-week cash-flow forecast prepared
- [ ] Local payment methods, fees, surcharge rules, and reminder-message consent requirements checked

## How to measure

Effective processing rate × monthly volume (contractual and easy to attribute — **a strong metric for a results-based bonus**); monthly financing or advance costs; reconciliation discrepancies recovered; interest and late fees paid.
