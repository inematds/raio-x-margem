# Module: Local marketing and community

> Addresses: **retention**, **marketplaces** and **marketing**. The advantage that no marketplace or SaaS platform can copy is **being part of the neighborhood**.

## The idea: the restaurant as a neighborhood gathering place

Marketplaces sell food. A neighborhood restaurant can offer **a sense of belonging**. People who feel part of something may be less likely to switch for a $5 app coupon.

## Layer 1 — the restaurant on its own

| Action | What it is | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Neighborhood club** | An opt-in SMS or email list for the restaurant, named for the neighborhood (for example, “Maple Street Club”) | Customers join for a benefit and stay for useful, relevant updates |
| **Vote on the weekly special** | Run a poll through a channel customers have agreed to receive messages on; put the winner on the menu | Involves customers, starts conversations and gives them a reason to check in |
| **Useful neighborhood news** | A local market, roadworks, a local team’s game or a school event | The channel becomes more than advertising |
| **Restaurant traditions** | A monthly neighborhood night, the restaurant’s anniversary or a special set menu | Creates a regular occasion |
| **Recognize customers by name** | A “customer of the week” board or post, with their permission | Makes customers feel recognized |
| **Neighbor referrals** | Give each customer a referral code; both people receive a benefit | Builds on local trust |

## Layer 2 — a network of neighborhood businesses

Cross-promote with **non-competing** businesses that serve the same audience: gyms, salons, pet shops, bakeries, language schools, coworking spaces and residential buildings.

- **Cross-promotions:** “Members of Gym X get a juice”; “Restaurant customers get a complimentary fitness assessment.”
- **Corporate lunches:** Set up arrangements with nearby companies and coworking spaces (see the [billing module](cobranca.md)).
- **Residential buildings:** Share the weekly menu in a building group or on its noticeboard with the property manager’s permission; group deliveries at a fixed time to reduce delivery costs.
- **Local causes:** Contribute a share of proceeds from a dish to a local school, team or nonprofit, and publish a clear account of the contribution.

## Layer 3 — the implementer as network owner (a business idea)

When an implementer serves **several businesses in the same area**, they can build something no single client can easily build alone:

**“Neighborhood Guide”** — a local webpage and channel, maintained by the implementer, featuring client restaurants’ current menus, partnership benefits and local events. For residents, it is a useful guide; for businesses, it brings shared traffic; for the implementer, it is **an asset of their own** that becomes more valuable with each new client and gives clients a reason to stay.

Use clear disclosure: participating businesses pay to be included, so the guide is not an impartial ranking. Limit each category to one client where businesses compete. Do not charge a commission per order or turn the guide into another marketplace.

## Implementation paths

**A — minimum:** An opt-in SMS or email list + a calendar of 4 updates per week + 2 partnerships. Follow local consent and privacy rules, which differ across the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia.

**B — complete:** Club webpage (sign-up with consent and benefits), a CRM messaging sequence ([acquisition](aquisicao.md)) and the Neighborhood Guide.

## Checklist

- [ ] Club name and promise (1 sentence)
- [ ] Sign-up benefit + loyalty rules
- [ ] First month’s calendar (daily special, poll, neighborhood news, restaurant tradition)
- [ ] Approach 3 nearby businesses about partnerships
- [ ] In-person sign-up point (QR code on the table, counter or restaurant-owned packaging)
- [ ] Starting baseline: members 0, member orders per week

## How to measure

Club members; percentage of members who ordered this month; orders using a partner or referral code; repeat order rate for member and non-member groups.
